## Shortest vectors as pairs of subsets

Let $p=523$ and $k\in\{4,5\}$. Use the coefficient model of the Craig lattice, the ideal generated by $(X-1)^k$ in $\mathbb Z[X]/(X^p-1)$. A vector $v$ is the coefficient list of $f(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}v_i X^i$, with squared norm $\sum_i v_i^2$.

Membership is equivalent to $\sum_i v_i=0$ over the integers and $\sum_i i^j v_i=0$ modulo $p$ for $1\le j<k$. To see this, put $y=X-1$ and reduce modulo $y^k$. The relation $(1+y)^p-1$ becomes $py$ times an integer polynomial with constant term 1, a unit in the truncated ring. Hence the constant Taylor coefficient must vanish integrally and the coefficients in degrees 1 through $k-1$ must vanish modulo $p$. The binomial polynomials $\binom{i}{j}$ giving those Taylor coefficients have invertible leading coefficients modulo $p$, so the power-sum conditions are equivalent. The congruences cut out a finite-index subgroup of the integer sum-zero lattice, of rank $p-1$.

Let $m$ be the total positive mass of a nonzero lattice vector. Its negative mass has the same magnitude. Each side gives a multiset of $m$ field elements. If $m<k$, Newton's identities recover the elementary symmetric functions from their first $m$ power sums since $m<p$. The two monic root polynomials, hence the two multisets, would coincide. Their supports are disjoint. Consequently $m\ge k$ and $\sum_i v_i^2\ge\sum_i|v_i|=2m\ge2k$. Equality requires exactly $k$ coordinates of each sign, each of magnitude 1.

Disjoint supports attain equality in both cases. For $k=4$, take $\{\pm1,\pm8\}$ and $\{\pm4,\pm7\}$. All odd moments vanish and $1+64=16+49$. For $k=5$, take $\{0,4,8,16,17\}$ and $\{1,2,10,14,18\}$. Both give the consecutive integer power sums 45, 625, 9,585 and 153,409.

It follows that shortest vectors correspond bijectively to ordered pairs of distinct $k$-subsets of $\mathbb F_p$ with identical first $k-1$ power sums. Such subsets are automatically disjoint. Removing one common point would leave two $(k-1)$-subsets with identical first $k-1$ power sums, so Newton's identities would make them equal.

## Counting the pairs

Subtract the common mean from both subsets. The binomial expansion preserves equality of all required moments. Each centered pair has exactly $p$ translates, one for each first power sum. For centered subsets, let $n$ be the number with a particular tuple of moments $(s_2,\ldots,s_{k-1})$ and let $H_n$ be the number of such tuples of multiplicity $n$, including unrealized moment tuples in $H_0$. The kissing number is $p\sum_n H_n n(n-1)$.

### Enumeration by polynomial values

A centered $k$-subset gives one monic polynomial with those distinct roots and vanishing degree-$(k-1)$ coefficient. By Newton's identities, fixing the required centered moments fixes every coefficient except the constant. Write $g$ for the polynomial with that constant set to zero. The group size $n$ is the number of field values with exactly $k$ preimages under $g$. Each such fiber is one distinct root set.

- For $k=4$, use $g=X^4+AX^2+BX$. Scaling all roots by $u\ne0$ sends $(A,B)$ to $(u^2 A,u^3 B)$. The script evaluates one representative per orbit and weights it by the number of distinct coefficient pairs in that orbit.
- For $k=5$, use $g=X^5+AX^3+BX^2+CX$. The scaling weights of $A,B,C$ are 2, 3 and 4. For fixed $A$, the histogram over all $B,C$ is constant within each square class of $A$, since scaling permutes the allowed $B,C$. Use $A=0,1$ and one nonsquare, with weights $1,261,261$. At fixed $A,C$, scaling by $-1$ exchanges $B$ and $-B$. Thus $B=0$ has weight 1 and each sign-pair representative has weight 2.

### Independent enumeration by subsets

For $k=4$, enumerate the sorted residues $a<b<c<d$ whose sum is $0$ in the field. Their ordinary sum is $p$, $2p$ or $3p$. Fixing $a,b$ and this sum determines $d$ from $c$ and supplies the loop bounds $b<c<d<p$. Count by the second and third power sums directly.

For $k=5$, enumerate the 23,434,580 five-subsets containing the two anchors 0 and 1. Center each and compute its three power sums. Group the resulting triples by the action $(a,b,c)\mapsto(u^2a,u^3b,u^4c)$ of nonzero scalars. The lookup table first groups $(a,b)$ by scaling. For each such representative, it groups $c$ using the scalars that fix $(a,b)$. This includes triples with zero components.

For one orbit of moment triples, write $h$ for the number of anchored sets in it and $s$ for the number of scalars fixing one triple. To count all sets in the orbit before centering, distinguish two ordered elements of each five-set. There are 20 choices. There is a unique affine change sending the distinguished elements to 0 and 1. Conversely, applying all $p(p-1)$ affine changes to anchored sets produces all the marked sets exactly once. Translation leaves centered moments unchanged, while scaling stays within the orbit. Thus there are $hp(p-1)/20$ sets before centering, and $h(p-1)/20$ centered sets. The orbit consists of $(p-1)/s$ moment triples with the same multiplicity. Each triple therefore has multiplicity $n=hs/20$. The script checks integrality in every orbit.
