# Cyclic length from the rotating complement in a regular wreath product

## Statement

Let $p$ be a prime and $a,k$ positive integers. Put $n=p^k$, and let
$G=(C_{p^a})^n\rtimes C_n$, where a generator $t$ of $C_n$ rotates
the $n$ coordinates in one cycle. Let $H=\langle t\rangle$. Among all
chains $H=K_0\triangleleft K_1\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft K_s=G$
with nontrivial cyclic successive quotients, the minimum length is

$$
s=n+(a-1)(n-n/p)=p^{k-1}\bigl(a(p-1)+1\bigr).
$$

This evaluates a family of instances of source 314, “Cyclic length and
chromatic fixed-point loss” (physical line 578 in the assigned INDEX.tsv).
Its local abstract identifies this minimum with the optimal chromatic loss.
If that theorem is used, the displayed value is the loss at every nonnegative
height. Only the group-theoretic statement is proved here. The source theorem
has not been independently checked. Novelty is UNVERIFIED.
The local abstract is in src/openai_math/CONTENTS.md, lines 7717–7726,
relative to the mining directory.

## Proof

Identify the additive base with the group ring
$R=(\mathbb Z/p^a)[t]/(t^n-1)$, using the $n$ coordinate vectors as
$1,t,\ldots,t^{n-1}$. Write $y=t-1$. Every intermediate subgroup is
$U\rtimes H$, where $U$ is its intersection with the base. Indeed, one
can remove the rotation component of any element by multiplying on the right
by an element of $H$. The additive subgroup $U$ is closed under rotation.
For two such subgroups with bases $U\subseteq V$, normality of the smaller
in the larger is equivalent to $yV\subseteq U$. Conjugating $t$ by
an element $v\in V$ gives $(v-tv)t$ in semidirect-product notation.
This tests all necessary conjugations because the base is abelian and $H$
preserves $U$.

Moving down any admissible chain therefore gives $y^s R=0$. Conversely,
take the bases $R,yR,y^2R,\ldots,0$. Consecutive terms give normal
inclusions. Each quotient $y^iR/y^{i+1}R$ is generated additively by
the class of $y^i$, since every polynomial multiplier reduces to its
constant term at $y=0$ modulo $y$. This additive quotient is also the
group quotient after adjoining $H$ to both bases. If $y$ is nilpotent,
no equality of consecutive nonzero ideals is possible, since multiplying
such an equality repeatedly by $y$ would give zero. Thus the desired
minimum is the first exponent $s$ with $y^s=0$.

Set $m=n/p$ and $d=n-m$. In the presentation
$R=(\mathbb Z/p^a)[y]/((1+y)^n-1)$, the powers $1,\ldots,y^{n-1}$
are a basis by monicity. To reduce a power $y^e$ with $e\ge n$, replace
$y^n$ by $-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom nj y^j$.
For $1\le j<n$, the valuation of $\binom nj$ is $k-v_p(j)$.
Indeed, $j\binom nj=n\binom{n-1}{j-1}$, and modulo $p$ the coefficients
of $(1+x)^{n-1}=(1+x^n)/(1+x)$ are alternating units.

Give a nonzero term $c y^e$ weight $d v_p(c)+e$, reducing coefficients
modulo $p^a$. A replacement never decreases weight, and only the summand
with $j=m$ preserves it. To see this, if $v_p(\binom nj)=1$, then
$j$ is a positive multiple of $m$, so $j+d\ge n$, with equality
only at $m$. If the valuation is at least two, then $j+2d>n$ since
$2d\ge n$. The exponent of $y$ strictly decreases at every replacement.

The greatest possible weight of a nonzero reduced monomial is
$W=(a-1)d+n-1$. Start with $y^{W+1}$. Every branch of its expansion
ends at a reduced monomial of weight at least $W+1$ or at a zero
coefficient. The former is impossible, so $y^{W+1}=0$.
Starting with $y^W$, the only branch that can survive chooses $j=m$
at every replacement. It decreases the exponent by $d$ each time and
after $a-1$ replacements gives
$(-\binom nm)^{a-1} y^{n-1}$. Its coefficient has valuation $a-1$,
so it is nonzero in the monic basis. All other branches increase weight
and vanish. Consequently $s=W+1$, as claimed.

The same value is also the nilpotency class of $G$. Indeed, the base and
the rotation subgroup are each abelian, and their commutators generate $yR$.
Commuting $y^iR$ with $G$ gives $y^{i+1}R$, so the lower central series
has last nontrivial term $y^{s-1}R$.

## Computation

In Python 3.11.9, the script checked the displayed formula for the last
nonzero power and the vanishing of the next power in 65 parameter triples
with $p\in\{2,3,5,7\}$, $1\le a\le5$, $1\le k\le4$, $p^k\le128$.
Separately, it searched for shortest chains by breadth-first search. For each
subgroup $K$, one representative $g$ of each left coset suffices. The test
$gK=Kg$ detects normality of $K$ in the extension generated by $K$ and $g$.
Every cyclic normal extension occurs this way by lifting a quotient generator.
The search uses only group multiplication.
