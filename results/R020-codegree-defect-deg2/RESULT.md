# A nonzero sparse Ramsey codegree defect has a vertex of degree at least three

## Theorem

Let G be a simple 22-regular graph on 45 vertices with no clique or independent set of size 5. Let A be its adjacency matrix, J the all-ones matrix, and D = A² + A − 11I − 11J. Assume the entries of D lie in {−2,−1,0,1} and, in each row, the entries on neighbors and on nonneighbors each sum to zero. Form the defect graph by joining i and j when Dᵢⱼ ≠ 0.

**If the defect graph has maximum degree at most two, D = 0.** Equivalently, a nonzero defect forces a vertex incident to at least three defective pairs.

This excludes an entire stratum of the nonzero-defect realization problem in rStation paper 1, §7 (INDEX.tsv line 645). Its §5 derives the stated hypotheses when every colored edge lies in at most two saturated triangles. The degree used here counts defective pairs per vertex; the source's saturation cap counts triangles per colored edge. Combining this theorem with the exclusion of D=0 in that saturation branch (paper 2, Theorem 6.5, imported and not reverified) makes the three-defective-pair conclusion unconditional within that branch. Existence in the higher-defect-degree branch is unresolved. Novelty is UNVERIFIED. [Paper 1, §§5 and 7]((local file)) and [paper 2, Theorem 6.5]((local file)).

## Why the two classes of each cycle are homogeneous across the cycle

D has zero diagonal by regularity. Every nonzero row of degree at most two has exactly two entries, +1 and −1. They have identical adjacency status by the separate zero sums. Thus every nontrivial component is even, has alternating signs, and all its cycle edges have a single color in G. Write its length as 2k, k ≥ 2.

Label its two parity classes U and V cyclically so that D from U to V is T = I−P, where P is a cyclic permutation matrix, and the reverse block is Tᵀ. Regularity gives AD = DA. Writing B for the adjacency block from U to V, the U,U block gives BTᵀ = TBᵀ. Average B over simultaneous cyclic shifts of its rows and columns and call the result C. It commutes with P and satisfies the same equation. Since Tᵀ = −P⁻¹T, we obtain T(Cᵀ+P⁻¹C) = 0. The matrix in parentheses is circulant and each column is constant, so it equals a scalar multiple of J. The entries of B on the matchings I and P all have the same value ε in {0,1}. Hence the diagonal entries of both Cᵀ and P⁻¹C are ε, making the scalar 2ε. Each summand has entries in [0,1], so all entries of C equal ε. Averaging binary entries can attain that endpoint only when every original entry equals ε. This proves the homogeneity assertion.

## Exact length filters

Let the half-lengths be k₁ through k_c and m = 2Σkᵢ. The kernel of D consists of vectors constant on each parity class, with arbitrary entries on isolates. This follows from ker(I−P) = ker(I−Pᵀ) = span{1} on each cycle block. Thus rank D = m−2c. Since A commutes with D, the image under A of each cell indicator is constant on every cell. This gives an equitable partition into parity classes and singleton isolates.

1. Put S = J−I−2A. Expansion gives S² = 45I−J+4D. The space W = im D is perpendicular to the ones vector, supported on the m cycle vertices, and invariant under S. The trace of D on W is its full trace, zero. Hence the squared Frobenius norm of S restricted to W is 45(m−2c). The principal submatrix of S on the m vertices restricts to that same operator on W and has squared Frobenius norm m(m−1). This proves m(m−1) ≥ 45(m−2c).
2. On a 2k-cycle the eigenvalue +2 of D occurs once if k is even, and never if k is odd. Indeed TTᵀ = 2I−P−Pᵀ has eigenvalue 4 exactly on the alternating vector when k is even. Each singular value 2 of T gives one +2 eigenvector of the symmetric off-diagonal block matrix D. On the rational +2 eigenspace, S² = 53I. Its dimension is even because x²−53 is irreducible over the rationals. Thus an even number of the kᵢ are even.
3. For one cell of size k and internal degree d, sum the Hamming distances of the adjacency rows for all unordered pairs x,y in the cell. Since Dₓᵧ = 0 their distance is 22+2Aₓᵧ, giving total 11k(k−1)+kd. A cell of size h with b neighbors in the chosen cell per vertex contributes h b(k−b). If the forward degree is a, then ka=hb. Thus (a,b) = (th/g,tk/g) for 0 ≤ t ≤ g = gcd(k,h). The own cell contributes k d(k−d), and its paired cell contributes zero distance and degree 0 or k by homogeneity. The singleton cells contribute zero distance and in total any degree from 0 to 45−m is an upper relaxation. The script keeps the achievable pairs (partial degree, partial distance sum), requiring degree 22 and the exact distance total. It also requires kd even and discards d = 0,k−1 when k ≥ 5 by the Ramsey condition.
4. For a block B from a size-k cell to a size-h cell, AD² = D²A gives (Pₖ+Pₖᵀ)B = B(Pₕ+Pₕᵀ) from the cycle formula in step 2. Expand the columns in Fourier modes. An h-mode of frequency angle θ can map only to k-modes with cosine cos θ, whose frequencies are θ or −θ modulo 2π. Their orders divide both h and k. Thus every column has period dividing gcd(k,h) in its k row indices. For two rows differing by step s in the k-cell, only cells with gcd(k,h) not dividing s can distinguish them. Isolates and the paired cell cannot distinguish them. This gives at most 2Σ h − k distinguishing positions, summing h over eligible cycles including the chosen one. This bound must be at least 22 for each 1 ≤ s < k, by the distance formula of step 3.
5. Every isolate sees whole nonsingleton cells. Its degree among the 45−m isolates is therefore 22 minus a subset sum of the cell sizes. Keep only permissible degrees from 0 to 44−m. If all are odd and the number of isolates is odd, the handshake lemma rules out the tuple. An empty set of permissible degrees is also impossible.

The script enumerates every nonempty nondecreasing tuple with kᵢ ≥ 2 and Σkᵢ ≤ 22. Starting from 1001 tuples, the five successive filters leave 687, 340, 6, 4 and 3. The surviving multisets of full lengths are eight 4-cycles, ten 4-cycles, and two 4-cycles with four 8-cycles. All computations use integers; the Fourier calculation is an exact proof of a divisibility test.

## Excluding the mixed cycle type

In each of the eight size-four cells use the two vectors e₀−e₂ and e₁−e₃. These 16 orthogonal vectors have equal length. Their span is invariant under A: it is the eigenvalue-2 space of D² (on a size-four cell D² is 2I−P−Pᵀ; on size-two cells its eigenvalues are 0 and 4). In this basis the representing matrix H is symmetric with integral entries in {0,±1}: the coefficient at a basis vector with first coordinate i of the image of eⱼ−eⱼ₊₂ is Aᵢⱼ−Aᵢ,ⱼ₊₂.

Within each 8-cycle the cross block acts by zero by homogeneity. The two internal adjacency blocks L,R are regular symmetric binary 4 by 4 matrices with zero diagonal, and LT=TR by commutation. Of the eight regular graphs on four labeled vertices exactly four ordered pairs (L,R) meet this condition: L=R is empty, the antipodal matching, its complement, or complete. The script checks all 64 pairs. Thus the within-cycle block of H is diagonal with entries 0 or −1. In contrast, the block of D between the two cells of that cycle on these bases is I minus the quarter-turn restricted to the two differences; all four entries are ±1.

On this span H²+H=11I+D (D here denotes its restriction). The diagonal entries of D vanish. Consequently each row of H contains eleven entries ±1 among its twelve positions outside its own cycle, and a single zero there. For two rows in opposite cells of the same cycle, the outside zeros occur in different columns: otherwise that column, also a row by symmetry, would have two outside zeros. The two rows thus have ten common nonzero positions outside the cycle, and their within-cycle entries have disjoint support. Their inner product is even. The same entry of 11I+D−H is ±1, a contradiction.

## Excluding eight 4-cycles

For c disjoint 4-cycles, use the vector e₀−e₁ on each of their 2c parity classes. They have equal norms and span im D. Order the basis with the first cell of every cycle first, followed by its partner. The matrix of D is twice the permutation exchanging partners. Since A commutes with D, its restriction has blocks [P,Q;Q,P], with P,Q symmetric, integral and bounded entrywise in absolute value by 1. The diagonal of P is 0 or −1 and that of Q vanishes by homogeneity. On the sum and difference spaces of the partners, A has matrices B₊ = P+Q and B₋ = P−Q. They satisfy B₊²+B₊=13I and B₋²+B₋=9I.

In any row let s be the number of odd off-diagonal entries, identical for both signs. Let a,b count off-diagonal entries of magnitude two in B₊,B₋ respectively. Those heavy entries have disjoint positions since P,Q have entries 0,±1. The diagonal equations give s+4a=13, s+4b=9. If c=8, then s+a+b ≤ 7 forces (s,a,b)=(1,3,2). The common odd positions form a perfect matching. Reduction of either quadratic identity modulo 2 on a matched pair forces its two diagonal entries to have opposite parities.

For each sign write B = Z+2H, with Z consisting of its diagonal and its signed matching entries only. Order each pair to put diagonal 0 first, −1 second. Each pair block of Z squares to I−Z. Therefore ZH+HZ+H vanishes modulo 2, since (13−1)/2 and (9−1)/2 are both even. Between two pairs any 2 by 2 block h of H modulo 2 must solve zh+hz+h=0 with z=[0,1;1,1]. Its three nonzero solutions in row-major order are 0110, 1011, 1101. Their supports intersect pairwise. Thus at most one sign can have a nonzero heavy block between any two pairs, and that block contains at most three entries. A matched pair needs 2(3+2)=10 heavy entries across both signs; its three other pairs supply at most nine. This excludes c=8.

## Excluding ten 4-cycles

Keep P,Q,B₊,B₋ from the eight-cycle argument, now of size 10. Both B matrices have the same diagonal, zero or −1. Since x²+x−13 is irreducible over the rationals, B₊ has each root with multiplicity five. Its trace −5 gives five diagonal entries of each kind. For c=10, the same row equations and s+a+b ≤ 9 allow only (s,a,b)=(1,3,2) or (5,2,1).

### The common pattern of odd entries

Reduce either matrix modulo 2 and place the five zero diagonal entries first. Write the resulting blocks as [R,C;Cᵀ,I+T], where R,T are adjacency matrices on five vertices. The quadratic equations become

    CCᵀ = R²+R+I,    CᵀC = T²+T+I,    RC = CT       (mod 2).

Every vertex has odd off-diagonal degree 1 or 5. The function odd_graphs in last_type.py enumerates all possibilities. It generates all 1024 labeled five-vertex graphs, retains one R per permutation orbit, and indexes all T by T²+T+I. It tries the 32 possible binary rows of C in turn, tests the row degree and the required inner products, and orders its five partial columns lexicographically. Simultaneous permutation of those columns and of T allows this ordering. Finally it checks the second Gram equation, the bottom row degrees, and RC=CT. The only odd-position graphs are a matching between the two five-vertex groups, or that matching together with all edges within both groups. In each case the matching can be labeled as the identity.

**Matching alone.** The argument with z and h for eight cycles still applies. At each matched pair there are four other pairs. Across their four blocks the pair needs ten heavy entries, partitioned into six of the plus sign and four of the minus sign. Every block has size 0, 2 or 3. A zero would give at most nine total. The only remaining multiset is 2,2,3,3. The two size-three blocks must be plus and the size-two blocks minus. Thus the minus blocks of size two form a 2-regular simple graph on the five pairs, a pentagon. Their only possible mask is 0110. Ordering the plus-diagonal-zero vertices first, B₋ therefore has the form [0,L;Lᵀ,−I], where L has signed diagonal entries of magnitude one and entries of magnitude two at the two pentagon neighbors in each row. The upper diagonal block equation gives LLᵀ=9I. Rows at distance two in the pentagon have just one common nonzero position, their common pentagon neighbor. Their dot product is ±4. This excludes the matching.

**Two five-cliques plus a matching.** Here every vertex has s=5. The heavy entries of B₋ make a matching disjoint from the odd positions, and those of B₊ have degree two. They all join the two five-vertex groups. With the odd matching fixed as the identity, the minus heavy matching is a derangement. Up to simultaneous relabeling of the groups, this is a 5-cycle or a 3-cycle and a transposition.

For either sign write the matrix in integer blocks [R,C;Cᵀ,S−I], where R and S are signed adjacency matrices of K₅. Normalize the four signs in the first row of R and the five diagonal signs of C to +1 by diagonal sign conjugation (one switch on each first-group position, followed by the corresponding second-group switches). The two signs can be normalized independently: on the sum and difference eigenvectors for one defect cycle an arbitrary pair of signs is induced by swapping its two cells, reversing the order inside both cells, or doing both. These relabelings preserve D. In the minus matrix C has one heavy entry per row at its derangement position. In the plus matrix C has two heavy entries per row, avoiding the diagonal and that derangement.

The function top_blocks tests all 64 normalized R choices. For each, it enumerates all signed rows of C with the specified heavy positions or exclusions and checks CCᵀ = nI−R²−R row by row, where n=13 or 9. It checks the heavy column counts. The function signed_blocks then tries all 1024 possible S and tests RC+CS=0. These necessary block equations suffice for the exclusion. For the 3+2 derangement zero plus matrices survive. For the 5-cycle derangement two plus matrices and one minus matrix survive.

### Ramsey test on the 40 cycle vertices

For each of the two combinations, recover P=(B₊+B₋)/2 and Q=(B₊−B₋)/2. In the two-vertex parity classes every binary biregular block is empty, complete, or one of the two perfect matchings. Its coefficient on the difference vectors is 0 in the first two cases and +1 or −1 in the latter two. The recovered matrices fix this coefficient for every pair of classes. Allocate one Boolean edge variable for each undetermined uniform block. The two vertices inside a cell are adjacent exactly when its diagonal coefficient is −1.

For every five-set on the 40 vertices forbid a clique and an independent set. A fixed opposite-color edge makes that clause unnecessary. The script also sets all uniform blocks empty, then complete, and verifies the prescribed action of the lifted 40-vertex adjacency matrix on every sum and difference vector in both controls. Otherwise the clause requires at least one involved variable to take the opposite color. Generation by recursive extension and by direct enumeration of all 658,008 five-sets produced identical clause sets. The two systems had 10,804 and 10,564 distinct clauses. Each was unsatisfiable both by a bitset DPLL search (95 and 55 nodes respectively) and by a separate literal-set DPLL implementation with simultaneous unit propagation. Branching on a variable covers both assignments; an empty clause certifies failure. This contradiction already holds on the 40 cycle vertices and completes the theorem.
